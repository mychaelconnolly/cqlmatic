- `import-parsers` and `fetch-parsers` no longer delete parser bindings, their line reviews or parser exclusions. 0.1.13 deleted each one whose exact reviewed bytes were missing from the new capture: after a capture that lacked its parser's detail, such as a fetch that ran out of time, after an `import-parsers` request with no pages and no details, and after the same parser was saved again as other bytes, such as with other key order or spacing. Now a binding or exclusion stays active when the capture saves the same bytes. When the capture saves the same parser resource as other bytes, with every field of the resource equal after JSON parsing, the review carries over, and `status.carried_parser_reviews` pairs the reviewed hash with the saved one. Otherwise the binding or exclusion is suspended: `status.suspended_parser_reviews` keeps it with its line reviews or reason, it counts for nothing, and the source's next actions say so. A later import or fetch that saves the same definition again makes it active with nothing re-entered. A changed parser still needs a fresh review, and binding the source again replaces its suspended binding. Both replies count the earlier parser bindings and exclusions in `parser_reviews` (`kept`, `carried`, `suspended`, `restored`) in full, summary and brief output, with a `next_action` when anything is suspended. An `import-parsers` request with no page and no detail is refused with exit code 2 and saves nothing. A state that holds a suspended or carried review cannot be read by 0.1.13 and earlier; other states are written as before. Saving an individual parser with `env parsers --id` preserves exact JSON number tokens. Restoring the same capture after a temporary gap preserves reconciliation and the order of carried or suspended reviews. Inventory status rejects contradictory definition digests for the same reviewed parser bytes.
